# The Outing & Travel Playbook™

## Keep the 4-Ring Defense™ With You — Cookouts, Camping, Sports, and Vacations

**Why this file exists:** The system protects your home — but mosquito-borne disease
doesn't respect property lines. The highest-risk exposures of the season often
happen *away* from home: the lakeside cookout, the soccer tournament at dusk, the
camping trip, the vacation to a region with active transmission. This playbook
gives you a pre-flight checklist for every common outing, so the system travels
with you in a duffel bag instead of staying home in the garage.

**The universal truth of off-property defense:** when you're away from home you
can't control the breeding — so Rings 1 (skin) and 3 (barriers you bring) carry
the load. Every playbook below is built on that.

---

## The Every-Outing Go-Bag (pack once, grab always)

Keep a dedicated zip pouch stocked all season:

- [ ] 1× full-size EPA-registered repellent (20–30% DEET or 20% picaridin)
- [ ] 1× kid-safe repellent (age-appropriate; see Tool #4)
- [ ] 1× travel-size repellent for pockets/purses
- [ ] Repellent wipes (easy reapplication, no spraying in the car)
- [ ] 1× permethrin-treated bandana or neck gaiter (great for hikes)
- [ ] After-bite relief: hydrocortisone cream + oral antihistamine (age-appropriate)
- [ ] Small first-aid: antiseptic wipes, bandages
- [ ] This playbook (or screenshots of the scenario cards from Tool #4)

**Stocked once in May, you never think about it again until October.**

## Playbook A — The Backyard Cookout / Party (at someone else's house)

**Before you go (2 minutes):**
- [ ] Apply repellent before you arrive (or as soon as you get there)
- [ ] Wear light-colored, loose clothing; if dusk is likely, add long sleeves/pants
- [ ] Pack the travel-size repellent + wipes in your pocket

**On arrival:**
- [ ] Scope the yard: standing water? Tall grass at the edges? (You're not being
      rude — you're being informed. Position yourself upwind of the nursery.)
- [ ] Sit near the grill smoke or the fan if there is one — airflow is your friend
- [ ] Keep the travel repellent in your pocket, not the car — reapply after 2 hours
      or after any sweating

**Kids at the party:**
- [ ] Reapply kid repellent before dusk hits (the golden hour for bites)
- [ ] Long pants for little legs if they'll be in grass at dusk
- [ ] Check them for bites before bed; treat any with the Part 1 protocol (Tool #6)

## Playbook B — The Camping Trip

**Before you leave (the night before):**
- [ ] **Permethrin-treat everything that will touch the outdoors:** tent (outside
      walls + floor edges), camp chairs, backpack exterior, a set of camp clothes,
      socks, and a hat. Treat 24–48h ahead so it dries fully (per label).
- [ ] Pack the full repellent stack (adult + kid versions, wipes)
- [ ] Pack after-bite relief + the Red-Flag Card (Tool #6)
- [ ] If staying in a cabin: check screens on arrival; ask for a fan if none

**At camp:**
- [ ] **Tent discipline:** keep the door zipped except when entering; open it fast
      and close it fast. (The "hold the flap open while I get my pillow" move is
      how the tent becomes a mosquito nightclub.)
- [ ] Set up camp **upwind** of water and away from marshy edges
- [ ] Dusk rule: repellent on *before* the sun drops, long sleeves/pants on,
      and treat the campfire seating area with a fan if you have power, or accept
      smoke as your (partial) ally — campfire smoke genuinely helps, though it's
      not sufficient alone
- [ ] Headlamp trick: use red mode if available — white light draws insects to you
- [ ] Morning sweep: check the tent interior and vestibule for hitchhikers before
      you unzip into the day (also check shoes and gear)

**If camping in a region with active West Nile or other mosquito-borne virus:**
- [ ] Bring a treated mosquito net for sleeping (the "pop-up" nets over sleeping
      bags or cots are cheap and definitive)
- [ ] Move the full protocol up a gear: repellent on *all* exposed skin all day,
      treated long clothes at dawn and dusk

## Playbook C — Sports, Tournaments & Outdoor Events

**The situation:** kids' soccer at 5:30 PM, evening concerts, outdoor movies —
all of them happen exactly when Aedes (daytime biters) and Culex (dusk biters)
are trading shifts.

- [ ] Repellent applied at home *before* you load the car (it needs 1–2 minutes
      to bind; and nobody sprays in the car)
- [ ] Reapply at the field/venue (wipes make this painless) — the first application
      does NOT last a 2-hour tournament
- [ ] Ankles and legs are the target zone for field-side biting — don't skip them
- [ ] For evening concerts/movies on grass: long pants + socks; bring the wipes;
      sit near airflow if possible
- [ ] Post-event: check kids for bites during the car ride home (before they
      scatter); treat bites immediately (Tool #6) so the itch never takes hold

## Playbook D — The Vacation (domestic or international)

**Before booking, 2 minutes of research that pays all trip:**
- [ ] Check CDC travel health notices for your destination (wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel)
      — mosquito-borne disease risk (dengue, West Nile, malaria, etc.) is listed
      per country/region. This changes your packing list, not your plans.

**Packing list (the full stack):**
- [ ] 30% DEET or 20% picaridin (full-size — travel sizes run out on day 3)
- [ ] Kid versions if traveling with children
- [ ] Permethrin-treated clothes for evenings (or treat at the destination per label
      — but treat at home and pack them; you want them dry and ready)
- [ ] Treated mosquito net if lodging may be unscreened (beach bungalows, jungle
      lodges, budget hotels) — a travel net weighs 1 lb and ends the worst-case
      scenario
- [ ] After-bite relief + Red-Flag Card + your travel insurance card

**At the destination:**
- [ ] **Room check on arrival (5 minutes):** screens intact? Gaps under the door?
      AC or fan available? (Run the fan at night if no AC — it doubles as a barrier.)
- [ ] Dawn and dusk = indoors or fully protected (the two daily windows matter in
      *every* mosquito region on earth)
- [ ] Reapply repellent on the schedule — vacation distraction is how people get
      bitten. Set a phone alarm if needed.
- [ ] If the destination has dengue/malaria risk, the evening protocol is
      non-negotiable: treated clothes + repellent + net, every single night
- [ ] Coming home: if you develop fever or flu-like symptoms within 2 weeks of
      returning from *any* mosquito-risk region, tell your provider where you
      traveled. That one sentence can change the diagnostic path entirely.

## Playbook E — The "I Forgot Everything" Emergency Kit

You're at the lake, the repellent is at home, and the sun is dropping. Do this:

1. **Cover skin:** long sleeves, long pants, socks — even improvised (hoodie +
   sweatpants + socks beats bare skin by a mile)
2. **Move:** get to airflow (breezy point, fan, or indoors)
3. **Avoid the worst spots:** don't sit at the water's edge or in the tall grass;
   sit upwind of both
4. **Make smoke your friend** if there's a fire
5. **Check for bites before bed** and treat any immediately (Tool #6)
6. **Buy the cheap stuff tomorrow:** at any pharmacy, a basic DEET or picaridin
   product costs less than the ice cream you'll buy on the way home. There is no
   excuse to run unarmed twice.

## The Family Duty Roster (who checks what)

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Gear Keeper** | Go-bag stocked + repellents full + wipes fresh (weekly check) |
| **Skin Boss** | Everyone's repellent applied before outings; reapplications on schedule |
| **Zipper Cop** | Tent/screen-door discipline (the kids LOVE assigning this to a parent) |
| **Bite Nurse** | Post-outing bite checks + treatment + Red-Flag monitoring |
| **Intel Officer** | County mosquito report checked weekly; travel notices before trips |

**Rotate weekly.** The system survives because the load is shared — and kids who
own a job protect themselves better than kids who are nagged.

## The One-Paragraph Summary

Your home runs the 4-Ring Defense™ — but the season's riskiest exposures happen
away from it. Pack the every-outing go-bag once, run the playbook that matches
your plan (cookout, camp, sports, or travel), and never leave home without the
two things that matter most: repellent you'll actually reapply, and permethrin-
treated clothes for the dusk windows. Off-property, Rings 1 and 3 are your whole
game — and they're enough.
