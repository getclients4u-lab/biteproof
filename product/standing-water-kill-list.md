# The Standing-Water Kill List™

## Drain It. Tilt It. Treat It. — The Weekly Execution Tool

**Why this list exists:** The Yard Audit (Tool #2) *found* your breeding sites.
This Kill List *ends* them. It is designed to be executed in under 30 minutes once
you know your property, and in under 10 minutes as a weekly maintenance sweep.

**The three verbs of mosquito control:**
1. **DRAIN** — pour it out, tip it over, poke a hole, put it away.
2. **TILT** — anything stored outside gets stored upside-down or covered.
3. **TREAT** — water you cannot drain (ponds, rain barrels, bird baths, ditches)
   gets a larvicide (BTI dunks or bits) per label. Treatment kills larvae *before*
   they become biting adults.

**The weekly rhythm:** Execute this list every 7 days during mosquito season, and
**always within 48 hours of a heavy rain** (rain creates new sites and refills old
ones). Ten minutes of prevention per week prevents thousands of mosquitoes per month.

## Zone 1 — House Perimeter (the highest-yield zone)

| Item | Action | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Flower pot saucers | Dump water; store saucers upside-down when pots are wet | Weekly + after rain |
| Pot rims & built-in bases | Tip pots to drain; elevate so water can't collect | Weekly |
| Buckets, cans, tools | Store indoors or upside-down; drill drainage holes | Once + weekly |
| Trash can lids | Store lids upside-down or bungee them on | Weekly |
| Wheelbarrow | Tip it upside-down when not in use | Weekly |
| Tarps & pool covers | Re-tighten so water runs off; pump off standing water | Weekly + after rain |
| Bird bath | Dump, **scrub the sides** (10 seconds), refill fresh | 2× per week |
| Pet water bowls | Refresh daily (mosquitoes need 5+ days — daily refresh wins) | Daily |
| Clogged gutters | Clean leaf dams; trim overhanging branches; consider gutter guards | Seasonally + after storms |
| Downspout pooling | Extend downspouts 4–6 ft from foundation; grade away from house | Once |
| AC condensate line | Ensure it drains freely; no pooling in the drip pan | Monthly |
| Children's toys | Store indoors or in a lidded bin; turn sand buckets upside-down | Weekly |
| Sandbox | Cover when not in use; rake and dry out if waterlogged | Weekly |
| Grill covers & drip trays | Shake off pooling; store covers so water runs off | Weekly |
| Fence/mailbox post caps | Drill ¼" drainage holes in hollow caps | Once |
| Outdoor furniture cup-holders | Drill drainage holes or cover furniture | Once |
| Umbrella bases | Drain; store base covered or upside-down | Weekly |
| Decorative rocks/edging hollows | Fill with gravel/sand or drill weep holes | Once |

**Zone 1 weekly time:** ~8 minutes

## Zone 4 — Permanent Water (Treat, Don't Drain)

These hold water on purpose. Your tool is **BTI** (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis)
— a naturally occurring bacterium that kills mosquito larvae but is safe for birds,
fish, pets, and beneficial insects when used per label. It comes as **dunks**
(floating "donuts," ~30 days of protection) and **bits** (granules for smaller sites).

| Water feature | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Bird bath / small fountain | Best: dump + scrub 2×/week (no treatment needed). If you travel: add a BTI bit |
| Ornamental pond | Add fish (guppies, goldfish, minnows eat larvae) + 1 dunk per label size; keep water moving with a pump/aerator (mosquitoes avoid moving water) |
| Rain barrel | **Seal all inlets with fine mesh** (mosquito-proof screen) so adults can't enter; treat the water with BTI bits monthly as backup; use the water regularly so it doesn't stagnate |
| Unused swimming pool | Green/unmaintained pools are disease factories — shock, cover, or drain; if kept, maintain chlorine + circulation |
| Pool cover water | Pump standing water off covers weekly or treat with BTI bits |
| Koi pond / livestock trough | Fish eat larvae; add aeration; treat troughs with BTI bits (safe for livestock per label) |
| Ditches / drainage basins | Treat with BTI dunks monthly during season; improve flow if possible |
| Decorative rain garden | If water stands >5 days, add BTI monthly or redesign for faster infiltration |

**Zone 4 time:** ~5 minutes + monthly dunk replacement

## The 10-Minute Rain Sweep (after every heavy rain)

Print this mini-routine. Tape it by the back door.

1. Walk the foundation line — dump saucers, buckets, toys, lids. *(3 min)*
2. Shake tarps, covers, furniture, umbrellas; re-tighten anything sagging. *(2 min)*
3. Check low spots and swales — note new puddles that last >24h. *(2 min)*
4. Clear gutter downspout outlets; confirm water runs away from the house. *(2 min)*
5. Treat any new permanent puddle with BTI bits. *(1 min)*

**Done. You just prevented the next generation.**

## What Success Looks Like

- **Week 1:** You eliminate 80–95% of your breeding sources. Bite pressure drops
  noticeably, but you may still see mosquitoes flying in from neighbors' yards.
- **Week 2–3:** With no new generations hatching on your property, evening counts
  drop dramatically. The mosquitoes you see are visitors, not residents.
- **Week 4+:** Your property is a net *sink*, not a source. Combined with Ring 1
  (personal protection) and Ring 3 (barriers), your family's exposure is a small
  fraction of the neighborhood baseline.

**Remember the compounding math:** one female mosquito lays 100–300 eggs per batch,
every few days, all season. Every nursery you kill is not one mosquito — it's
**thousands per month** that will never exist. That is the power of the Kill List.
