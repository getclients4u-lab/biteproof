# The Home Barrier Blueprint™

## Seal the Structure, Defeat the Breeze, and Know What's Worth Buying

**Why this file exists:** Ring 3 is the layer that keeps mosquitoes from converting
your home into their evening buffet. Most homes have a handful of small failures —
a torn screen here, a gap under the door there — that let dozens of mosquitoes in
every night. This blueprint finds those failures, fixes them in an afternoon, and
then settles the "which gadget actually works?" question permanently.

**The three jobs of Ring 3:**
1. **Keep them out** (screens, seals, sweeps, and the dusk door protocol)
2. **Defeat them at the door** (airflow, lighting, and entryway design)
3. **Stop the indoor nonsense** (what works indoors, what's a scam)

---

## Part 1 — The Structure Audit (one afternoon, ~2 hours)

### The screen check (45 minutes)

A 16×16-mesh screen keeps mosquitoes out. A hole the size of a pencil eraser does not.

**Walk every window and door screen:**
- [ ] Look for holes, tears, and rust spots at corners and edges
- [ ] Press gently on each screen — old screens sag and pull free of the frame
- [ ] Check the screen-to-frame seal: gaps here are as bad as holes
- [ ] Check **tracks** — screens that don't seat fully leave a gap at top or bottom
- [ ] Test each door screen: does it close fully? Self-close? Seat flush?

**The fixes:**
| Problem | Fix | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Small hole/tear | Screen repair kit (patch) — 10 minutes | ~$8 |
| Sagging or loose screen | Re-spline the screen into the frame (kit: spline + roller) | ~$15 |
| Rotted/damaged frame | Replace the screen frame (standard sizes at any hardware store) | ~$20 |
| Screen doesn't seat | Adjust tracks; add weatherstrip to close the gap | ~$10 |
| No screen at all (high-risk window) | Install screen kit or window screen mesh | ~$25 |

**The pencil test:** after repairs, poke each screen gently with a pencil.
If the pencil goes through, mosquitoes can too. Fix it.

### The door sweep check (30 minutes)

Mosquitoes are ground-level fliers. The gap under an exterior door is a superhighway.

- [ ] Check the gap under every exterior door: a ¼" gap is enough for Culex
- [ ] Install or replace **door sweeps** (foam or brush type) — under $15 each
- [ ] Check weatherstripping around door frames — replace cracked/dried strips
- [ ] Check the **garage-to-house door** (the one people forget — mosquitoes follow
      the light into the garage, then into the house)
- [ ] Check pet doors: if you have one, make sure it seals when not in use

### The penetration check (30 minutes)

Mosquitoes enter through *any* hole. Walk the perimeter and seal:

- [ ] Gaps around AC line-sets entering the wall (foam sealant or steel wool + caulk)
- [ ] Utility penetrations: cable, internet, gas, electrical (same treatment)
- [ ] Gaps around outdoor spigots and hose bibs
- [ ] Dryer vents and bath fans: confirm dampers close fully when off
- [ ] Attic and crawlspace vents: check for torn screening
- [ ] Chimney: confirm the damper seals; consider a chimney cap
- [ ] Mail slots and transom gaps
- [ ] Crawlspace door / bulkhead: seal gaps (also helps humidity — bonus)

**Sealant rule:** exterior-grade caulk or expanding foam for gaps >¼"; silicone
caulk for smaller cracks. 30 minutes of sealing = hundreds of fewer mosquitoes.

## Part 2 — The Dusk Protocol (free, and huge)

Most indoor mosquito incursions happen in a 90-minute window around dusk, when
Culex mosquitoes are most active and lights are on inside. Install this as a
household habit:

1. **Doors closed at dusk.** If you're coming and going, make it a rule: nobody
   holds the door open at dusk. (Kids + screen doors = mosquitoes. Train early.)
2. **Lights strategy:** mosquitoes aren't strongly attracted to most household
   lighting, but they *are* attracted to the warm bodies near it. Draw blinds in
   rooms with lights on at dusk if windows are unscreened or frequently opened.
3. **The garage rule:** if the garage light is on and the door is open at dusk,
   you're running a mosquito collection service. Close the big door at dusk or
   accept the garage as a buffer zone — and keep the house door from the garage shut.
4. **The welcome-mat trap:** people standing at the open door chatting = mosquitoes
   streaming in. Move conversations inside or to the screened porch.
5. **Check before bed:** a 10-second sweep of bedrooms (especially kids' rooms) —
   look on walls and ceilings near windows. One mosquito in a bedroom = one night
   of lost sleep and one fresh bite in the morning.

## Part 3 — Fans: The Barrier You Can Feel

Mosquitoes are weak fliers. Sustained air movement of roughly **10–15 mph (about
the setting of a typical patio fan on medium)** makes it physically impossible for
them to fly and land effectively. This is not a gimmick — it's aerodynamics.

**How to deploy fans for defense:**
- **Patio/deck seating:** one fan per seating cluster, aimed across the table at
  roughly torso height. You'll notice the difference immediately.
- **Entryways:** a fan inside the door, angled at the threshold, creates an
  invisible curtain mosquitoes won't cross. (This is a well-known trick in
  mosquito-heavy regions — screened porches often have a fan at the door for this.)
- **Outdoor dining:** clip-on or pedestal fans under the table area defend the
  ankles — where Aedes bites.
- **Sleeping porches / gazebos:** a ceiling fan on medium makes the space
  effectively mosquito-free for sitting.

**Why fans beat sprays for seating areas:** no chemicals, no reapplication, works
the whole evening, and it cools you too. Fans are the highest "pleasure-per-dollar"
item in the entire BiteProof system.

## Part 4 — Outdoor Living Zones (patio, porch, deck, gazebo)

If you have a dedicated outdoor living area, upgrade it into a **defended zone**:

1. **Screened porch / gazebo:** fix all screening (Part 1) and add a fan. This
   becomes your dusk room — the place you actually use in mosquito season.
2. **Open patio/deck:** fans + the 20-foot harbor rule (trim shrubs and grass within
   20 feet of seating — see Tool #2) + treat the perimeter with an EPA-registered
   outdoor space spray **only when hosting** (per label; these kill on contact but
   don't last — they're for event prep, not weekly use).
3. **The hosting protocol (for parties at dusk):**
   - 30 minutes before guests arrive: apply an EPA-registered outdoor fogger/spray
     to the yard perimeter, under the deck, and around seating (per label)
   - Turn on all fans
   - Set out repellent wipes/lotion for guests who forgot (they'll remember you
     fondly)
   - Dump any water that appeared since the morning sweep
   - Move the party inside or to the screened area at full dark if pressure is high
4. **Lighting:** swap bright white patio lights for warm/yellow tones where possible
   — they're less attractive to insects. (Not a defense by itself, but it reduces
   the congregation effect near seating.)

## Part 5 — Indoor Defense (the honest version)

Mosquitoes *inside* came from somewhere: an open door, a torn screen, or they rode
in with you. Indoor defense is therefore 90% prevention:

**What works indoors:**
- The dusk protocol (Part 2)
- Fixed screens + sealed penetrations (Part 1)
- A **simple flyswatter or electric racket** for the occasional intruder
  (a 5-minute dusk sweep of bedrooms catches most)
- **Vacuum trick:** a mosquito on the ceiling → vacuum cleaner hose. Instant,
  zero-mess removal. (Yes, this is the pro move.)
- If you have a recurring indoor problem (e.g., mosquitoes breeding in a
  houseplant saucer, under the sink, or in a floor drain), find the water:
  houseplant saucers are the #1 indoor breeding site. Dump them weekly.

**What's a scam indoors:**
- UV "bug zapper" plug-ins: kill almost no mosquitoes (see myth table in Tool #4)
- Ultrasonic emitters: no evidence
- Citronella plug-ins: negligible effect indoors
- Any "whole-room" natural-oil diffuser claiming mosquito control: not supported

**The one legitimate indoor chemical tool:** an EPA-registered indoor flying-insect
spray (per label) for the rare active intruder — spray toward the insect, ventilate
the room. For almost everyone, the swatter/vacuum is enough.

## Part 6 — The Seasonal Structure Checklist

Run this once in late spring and once in late summer (peak season):

- [ ] All screens patched/replaced and seating properly in tracks
- [ ] All door sweeps sealing; weatherstripping replaced where cracked
- [ ] Every utility penetration sealed
- [ ] Garage-to-house door sealed; garage door closed at dusk
- [ ] Fans placed and tested in all seating areas + entryways
- [ ] Dusk protocol posted on the fridge / family calendar
- [ ] Outdoor hosting kit stocked (spray, wipes, spare repellent)
- [ ] Indoor plant saucers on the weekly dump list
- [ ] Bedroom evening sweep added to the nightly wind-down

## The One-Paragraph Summary

Mosquitoes get inside through small failures — a torn screen, a door gap, an open
door at dusk — and once inside, they ruin nights and spread bites. Fix the structure
once (an afternoon of screens, sweeps, and seals), install the dusk door protocol,
deploy fans as living barriers in every seating area, and refuse to waste money on
zappers and ultrasonic gadgets that the evidence doesn't support. Ring 3 turns your
home from a target into a fortress — quietly, cheaply, and permanently.
