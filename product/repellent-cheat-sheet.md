# The Repellent Cheat Sheet™

## Pick the Right Protection in 10 Seconds — For Every Person, Place & Situation

**Why this sheet exists:** The #1 reason repellents "don't work" is using the wrong
one for the situation — or using it wrong. This cheat sheet gives you the
decision tree, the active-ingredient decode, the application rules, and the
kid/pregnancy/sensitive-skin versions. Print it. Tape it inside a cabinet. End the
arguments.

**The foundation rule:** Only use **EPA-registered** repellents. The EPA has reviewed
them for safety and effectiveness. "Natural" is not a synonym for "tested" — see the
myth table at the end. The four active ingredients with the strongest track records
are **DEET**, **picaridin**, **IR3535**, and **oil of lemon eucalyptus (OLE/PMD)**.
A fifth, **2-undecanone**, is plant-derived and EPA-registered. All five work; the
"best" one is the one you'll actually use correctly and consistently.

---

## The 10-Second Decision Tree

**Start here every time you head out:**

1. **Who is it for?**
   - Adult → any EPA-registered active below
   - Child 2 months–3 years → DEET ≤30% or picaridin ≤20% (NOT OLE — see below)
   - Baby under 2 months → **no repellent** — use clothing, netting, and timing
   - Pregnant/nursing → DEET and picaridin are the most-studied choices; follow
     label and your provider's guidance
   - Sensitive skin → picaridin or IR3535 (less likely to irritate)
2. **What's the situation?**
   - Casual evening on the patio, low mosquito pressure → 10–20% concentration
   - Dusk/near water/known virus activity/hiking → 20–30% concentration
   - All-day exposure, heavy sweat, tropics, deep woods → 30% + permethrin clothing
3. **How long will you be out?**
   - ~2 hours → 10–15% product
   - ~4–6 hours → 20–25% product
   - 8+ hours or overnight → 30% product + treated clothing (reapply per label)
4. **Does the label say reapply? Then reapply.** Sweat, swimming, and time break
   down every repellent. Set a phone timer if you have to.

**Rule of thumb:** Higher concentration = longer protection, not stronger protection.
20–30% DEET covers virtually every civilian situation. You rarely need more.

## The Active Ingredient Decode

| Active | Typical protection | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DEET** (10–30%) | 2–8 hrs depending on % | The gold standard; ticks too | Most-studied repellent on earth; safe per EPA when used per label; can feel oily; damages some plastics/synthetics (wash hands after applying) |
| **Picaridin** (10–20%) | 3–8 hrs | All-day wear, sensitive skin | Odorless, non-greasy, won't damage gear; excellent all-rounder; the "modern default" |
| **IR3535** (10–20%) | 2–4 hrs | Sensitive skin, kids | Gentle; also repels biting flies; shorter duration — reapply more often |
| **Oil of lemon eucalyptus / PMD** (10–30%) | 2–6 hrs | Adults who prefer plant-derived | **Not for children under 3 years.** Perfomance similar to lower-% DEET; smells lemony |
| **2-undecanone** (5–10%) | 2–4 hrs | Plant-derived option | Derived from wild tomato; shorter duration; reapply per label |
| **Permethrin** (clothing only) | Weeks (through ~6 washes) | The invisible layer | **Never on skin.** Treat clothes/gear outdoors, let dry 2–4 hrs. Kills/repels mosquitoes AND ticks on contact. The single most underused tool |

**Why permethrin is the cheat code:** repellents fail when you forget to apply them,
miss a spot, or sweat them off. Permethrin-treated clothing works whether you remember
or not — every sleeve, collar, and sock is defended for the whole season. Treat a
few shirts, pants, and socks once, and your Ring 1 defense becomes automatic.

## Application Rules That Actually Matter

**Do:**
- Spray or lotion on **exposed skin only** — not under clothing
- Use **just enough to cover** — a thin, even layer; more is not better
- **Pay attention to ankles, feet, backs of knees, wrists, neck, and ears** —
  Aedes mosquitoes bite low; this is where most people get bitten through "protection"
- Apply **lotion first, then sunscreen** (sunscreen first, repellent on top)
- Wash repellent off with soap and water when you come inside
- Store out of reach of children

**Don't:**
- Spray directly on the face — spray hands, then pat on face/neck
- Apply to cuts, wounds, or sunburned skin
- Let young children apply their own
- Spray under clothing
- Use combined sunscreen+repellent products (sunscreen needs reapplication every
  2 hrs; repellent doesn't — you'd over-apply one or under-apply the other)

**Kids' rules:**
- Under 2 months: **no repellent** — stroller nets, long clothing, avoid peak times
- 2 months+: DEET ≤30% or picaridin per label; apply yourself — avoid hands/eyes
- Under 3 years: **no oil of lemon eucalyptus/PMD**
- Wash kids' skin with soap and water when back inside; wash treated clothing separately

## Scenario Cards

**🏡 Backyard barbecue, 6 PM, light pressure**
Adult: 20% picaridin or 20% DEET on exposed skin. Add a fan near the table (Ring 3).
Kids: 10–20% picaridin. Done in 30 seconds.

**🌅 Evening walk at dusk, near a creek, West Nile activity reported in county**
30% DEET or 20% picaridin + long pants/sleeves (light colors) + permethrin-treated
socks and shoes. Reapply if out past 90 minutes.

**⚽ Kids' soccer practice, 5:30 PM, field edge near trees**
Kids: 20% picaridin (less greasy, won't bother them). Spray ankles and legs —
grass-level Aedes are the field's real residents. Pack a wipe for reapplication.

**🏕️ Camping trip, all weekend**
Permethrin-treated clothes (done at home) + 30% DEET for face/hands + treat the
tent perimeter and camp chairs. Keep the tent zipped; check the vestibule at dusk.

**🌴 Vacation to a dengue/West Nile-active region**
The full stack: 30% DEET or 20% picaridin reapplied on schedule, permethrin-treated
clothing for evenings, long sleeves at dawn/dusk, treated mosquito net over the bed
if lodging isn't screened, and EPA-registered insecticide (per label) for the room.

**🤰 Pregnant or nursing**
DEET and picaridin are the most thoroughly studied repellents across decades of use,
including during pregnancy — far more data than any "natural" alternative. Per CDC
guidance, use per label; when in doubt, talk to your provider. Clothing + timing
adds protection without chemicals.

**🩹 Sensitive skin / eczema**
Picaridin or IR3535 lotion formulas (less irritating than sprays). Test a small area
first. Long, loose clothing + permethrin gear reduces how much repellent you need.

**🐕 The dog?** Mosquito repellents made for humans are **not** for pets. Ask your
vet about pet-safe options (many heartworm preventives are mosquito-related — keep
pets on their prescribed prevention). Don't spray DEET on animals.

## The Myth Table (What NOT to Waste Money On)

| Product | Verdict | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Citronella candles | **Weak** | Reduce bites marginally (some studies: ~20-30% in still air, near-zero outdoors with any breeze). Fine ambience, not defense |
| Ultrasonic / phone apps | **Hype** | No credible evidence any frequency repels mosquitoes. Zero |
| Wristbands (citronella/plant oils) | **Hype** | Protect a few square inches of wrist — and nothing else. Studies show no bite reduction |
| Vitamin B1 / garlic ingestion | **Hype** | Repeated studies show no effect on attractiveness |
| Bug zappers | **Mostly useless for mosquitoes** | Kill thousands of harmless insects; mosquitoes (attracted to CO2, not UV) make up a tiny fraction of catches. Some studies show zappers can even attract more mosquitoes to the area |
| Mosquito-repelling plants (citronella grass, lavender, marigolds) | **Weak** | The oils repel when concentrated & applied; the live plant in the garden does essentially nothing for the air around it |
| Thermacell & similar | **Legit, situational** | Creates a localized zone of protection (methylated oil + allethrin) — genuinely useful for patios in still air; not a full-yard solution; follow label |
| CO2 traps | **Legit, as part of a system** | Reduce local pressure over weeks; not instant; best placed away from seating areas (they attract mosquitoes toward the trap — put them at the property edge) |

**The money rule:** buy one good repellent per person-type in your house, one bottle
of permethrin, and BTI dunks. Everything else is optional. This system costs less
than one season of gadget purchases — and actually works.

## Your 60-Second Prep Routine (before any outdoor time)

1. Check the county mosquito report (30 seconds on your phone).
2. Pick the scenario card above that matches.
3. Apply repellent to exposed skin — ankles, legs, arms, neck. (Or confirm the
   permethrin-treated shirt is on.)
4. Set a reapplication timer if you'll be out past the product's duration.
5. Walk out the door protected — and actually enjoy the evening.

---

## When Repellent Isn't Enough

Repellent is Ring 1 — personal protection. If you're doing everything right and
still getting bitten, the problem is *pressure*: mosquitoes are breeding near you.
That's a Yard problem (Tools #2 and #3), not a skin problem. Run the system, not
just the spray.
