# The 15-Minute Yard Audit™

## Find Every Mosquito Nursery on Your Property — In One Pass

**Why this worksheet exists:** You cannot fix what you haven't found. Most people
believe they "don't have standing water" because they don't have a pond. Meanwhile
their property quietly hosts 40 breeding sites in a saucer, a tarp fold, and a
children's toy. This audit finds them all in a single 15-minute loop.

**How to run it:** Print this sheet (or open it on your phone). Walk the property in
the numbered zones below, in order, and check off every item you find. Don't fix
anything during the audit — just find and mark. Then execute the Standing-Water Kill
List (Tool #3) to eliminate what you marked.

**The master rule:** If it can hold water for **5 days**, it is a nursery.
Aedes mosquitoes breed in a bottle cap's worth. Culex prefers larger, dirtier water.
Both are covered below.

**Best time to audit:** Mid-morning on a dry day — after dew evaporates, every puddle
that remains is a true breeding source. Then audit again 24 hours after the next
heavy rain; that second pass catches the rain-created sites.

## Zone 1 — The House Perimeter (3 minutes)

Walk the entire foundation line, slowly, looking up and down.

**Checklist — mark everything you find:**
- [ ] Clogged gutters or downspouts (leaf dams hold water for weeks — a top Culex site)
- [ ] Downspout extensions that pool water at the foundation
- [ ] Gaps where the downspout meets the gutter (drips into the wall cavity)
- [ ] AC condensate drip pan / line pooling water
- [ ] Flower pot saucers (the #1 Aedes nursery in America)
- [ ] Pot bases with built-in rims that hold water
- [ ] Buckets, cans, or tools left outside
- [ ] Trash can lids (inverted lids = perfect water bowls)
- [ ] Wheelbarrows (the classic forgotten nursery)
- [ ] Tarps or pool covers with sagging folds holding rain
- [ ] Bird bath (needs a weekly scrub-and-refill — see Kill List)
- [ ] Pet water bowls left out for days
- [ ] Garden hose connections or hose-end sprayers holding water
- [ ] Children's outdoor toys: sand buckets, shovels, play kitchens, toy cars
- [ ] Sandbox (if uncovered and waterlogged, the liner can pool)
- [ ] Decorative rocks or landscape edging with hollows
- [ ] Stepping stones with depressions
- [ ] Outdoor furniture: chair arms with cup holders, table umbrellas (base collects water)
- [ ] Grill covers with pooling folds; grill drip trays
- [ ] Mailbox post caps, fence post caps, decorative post tops
- [ ] Light fixtures or outlet covers that collect water

## Zone 2 — The Yard & Lawn (4 minutes)

Walk a zigzag across the entire lawn and planted areas.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Low spots in the lawn that pond after rain (note: fix drainage or treat monthly)
- [ ] Tree holes / branch crotches that collect water (old trees with cavities)
- [ ] Fallen leaves piled in depressions (hold moisture + organic matter = Culex heaven)
- [ ] Tall grass or overgrown shrubbery touching the ground (adult resting harbor —
      mosquitoes hide in cool, damp vegetation during the day)
- [ ] Ivy or groundcover thickets near seating areas (same harbor problem)
- [ ] Dense shrub lines along fences (resting sites + windbreaks for mosquitoes)
- [ ] Weedy ditches or drainage swales that hold water
- [ ] French drain outlets that stay wet
- [ ] Compost piles that are too wet (turn them; cover if possible)
- [ ] Garden hoses left coiled with water inside (drain them fully)
- [ ] Irrigation heads leaking or pooling; valve boxes full of water
- [ ] Rain chains or decorative water features that splash and pool

**Zone 2 tally:** ______ sites found

## Zone 3 — Outbuildings, Storage & The "Back 40" (4 minutes)

Sheds, garages, woodpiles, fences, and the forgotten corners.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Open trash cans, recycling bins, or lidless containers
- [ ] Old tires (the single worst container mosquito habitat — a tire holds water for
      months and is a documented disease-vector breeder; drill holes or dispose)
- [ ] Anything stored upside-down that can catch water when flipped by wind/animals
- [ ] Shed gutters (yes, sheds have gutters — check them)
- [ ] Shed roof valleys or flat-roof pools
- [ ] Construction debris, buckets of dried paint, plant pots stacked outside
- [ ] Firewood stacks with hollow ends facing up (drill/tilt or cover)
- [ ] Wheelbarrow or cart tires holding water in the rim
- [ ] Boats, kayaks, or canoes uncovered (rain pools in the hull; cover or tilt)
- [ ] Pool toys, floats, and covers stored outside
- [ ] Septic tank risers or utility box lids with pooling water
- [ ] Fence post holes or gate posts with water at the base
- [ ] Abandoned appliances or furniture awaiting disposal
- [ ] Children's playsets: tire swings (drain holes), plastic cups in sand areas,
      slide platforms that pool

**Zone 3 tally:** ______ sites found

## Zone 4 — Water Features & Permanent Water (4 minutes)

These can't always be drained — so they get a *treatment* protocol instead.

**Checklist — for each, note the type:**
- [ ] Ornamental pond / water garden
- [ ] Rain barrel (unsealed openings breed Culex; see Kill List for the fix)
- [ ] Bird bath / fountain
- [ ] Swimming pool (check: is it circulating and chlorinated? Unmaintained pools
      become massive breeders; green pools are emergencies)
- [ ] Pool cover (standing water on top = nursery; pump it off weekly)
- [ ] Koi pond / livestock trough
- [ ] Decorative wetland / rain garden (if it holds water >5 days, treat with BTI)
- [ ] Natural drainage basin or ditch that holds water
- [ ] Neighbor-adjacent sources you can see from your property line
      (note them — you may need a diplomatic conversation or extra personal protection)

**Zone 4 tally:** ______ sites found

## Zone 5 — The Harbor Check (adult resting & hiding spots)

Mosquitoes don't just breed on your property — they *rest* there during the day and
launch from there at dusk. Circle the harbors near your outdoor living areas:

- [ ] Under the deck or porch (dark, damp, sheltered)
- [ ] Under outdoor stairs
- [ ] Dense shrubs within 20 feet of the door or patio
- [ ] Tall grass along the fence line
- [ ] The space behind the AC unit or meter box
- [ ] Under patio furniture when not in use
- [ ] Inside the grill cart or storage bench
- [ ] Garage with the door left open at dusk

**Harbor rule:** reduce harbors within 20 feet of where people sit. Trim, thin, and
create airflow. (Fans in seating areas defeat the rest — see Tool #5.)

**Scorecard:**
- 0–5 sites: You're in great shape. Weekly maintenance keeps it that way.
- 6–15 sites: Typical American yard. One solid Kill List session fixes most of it.
- 16–30 sites: You have a working nursery. Don't panic — the Kill List is designed
  for exactly this. Do it in two passes this week.
- 30+ sites: Record year, record yard. Consider the full 2-hour deep clean, then
  weekly maintenance. You'll be amazed at the difference by week two.

## Pro Tips From the Field

1. **Photograph everything.** Take a photo of each site as you find it. The photos
   become your Kill List "before" shots and your Week 4 "after" proof.
2. **Audit in the rain.** One rainy-day walk reveals the low spots and drainage
   failures that dry days hide. Do a quick rain audit once.
3. **Mind the neighbor factor.** Mosquitoes fly 100–200 yards from their breeding
   site (some species up to a mile). If you're diligent and still getting bites,
   the source may be next door. You can't control it — but you can escalate your
   personal protection (Ring 1) and your barrier defense (Ring 3) to compensate.
4. **Re-audit monthly.** Seasons change, storms move debris, and new items arrive in
   the yard. The 15-minute audit is a monthly habit, not a one-time event.
5. **After any heavy rain: the 10-minute sweep.** You don't need the full audit —
   just walk Zones 1 and 3, dumping and tilting as you go. This single habit
   prevents 80% of rain-created breeding.
